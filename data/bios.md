# Bryan

Bryan is the WFFL's winningest manager of all time — 103-82-2 across all 14 seasons the league has existed, a .556 clip that leads the league outright. Two championships bookend his run, six years apart (2018 and 2024), alongside a runner-up finish in 2019 and three trips to the third-place game (2014, 2015, 2017) — the kind of year-in, year-out contention that separates a great manager from a lucky one. He also owns the league record for most points in a single season, torching the field for 2,025.74 points in 2022, a mark that still stands. His signature win: an 83-point blowout of Steve to close the 2024 title run, 164.48-80.72. His signature scar: a 100-point beatdown at the hands of Lou in Week 4 of 2025, still the worst loss of his career. All-time, he's had Tim's number (13-5) but has never quite figured out Luke (7-8), the rare manager with a winning edge on him.

# Brendan

Brendan has been the WFFL's proof that close doesn't count — two runner-up finishes (2012, 2014) and a third-place trip (2022), but 14 seasons in, the championship banner remains the one thing missing from his rafter. The numbers explain some of it: at 112.9 points per game he's a shade below the league average, and a 9-game losing streak somewhere in his history is the kind of stretch nobody wants next to their name. But he's not without teeth — a 9-4 all-time record against Steve is one of the more lopsided rivalries in the league, and his career-high 200.16 points in a single week (2022) is nothing to sneeze at. Somewhere out there, a title is waiting for him to actually finish the job.

# Ken

Ken missed the league's inaugural season but has more than made up for it since — a championship in 2021 proved he can get it done when it counts, even if a 2-4 career playoff record suggests October and November are usually a struggle before that magic shows up. His numbers are almost aggressively average — 82-92 for his career, points per game just a hair below league median — but the game logs tell a spicier story: a 183-71 demolition of Lefty in 2018 sits right next to a 55-166 no-show against Ted in 2016, the widest gap between a manager's best and worst weeks in the league. Ken's ceiling and floor are both real; the trick is knowing which one shows up on Sunday.

# Ted

Ted is the winningest champion in WFFL history — three titles (2016, 2017, 2022) and a 10-5 playoff record that says the regular season is just a formality for him. He also owns the league's all-time nine-game win streak and its single biggest blowout on record: a 150-32 demolition of Dave in 2018 that's still the most lopsided final score in 14 years. The one blemish on an otherwise loaded résumé: Lou has somehow gone 16-9 against him lifetime, the rare manager who's figured out the formula. Three rings say it hasn't mattered much.

# Luke

Luke is proof fantasy football rewards patience — a 15-game losing streak somewhere in his 14 seasons is the longest skid in league history, and yet he's got a championship (2023) and a runner-up finish (2020) to show for sticking it out. The turnaround was real: a 181-92 win over Mark in 2019 and a 185-point outburst in 2020 show the ceiling was always there. He owns a comfortable 12-6 edge on Justin, though Dave has had his number more often than not lifetime. Whatever the low point looked like, Luke's the only manager in the league who can say he won it all after living through a 15-game losing streak.

# Steve

Steve owns the single highest-scoring week in league history — 210.3 points in 2019 — and it powered him to a title that same year. He's a certified above-average scorer (120.6 points per game, among the league's best) with a dominant 16-7 all-time record against Tim. The one gap on the résumé: a runner-up finish in 2024 that came at the hands of Bryan, an 83-point beatdown he'd probably like to forget, and Mark has quietly gone 10-4 against him lifetime. Force of nature most weeks, but not immune to a bad night.

# Tim

Tim has a puzzling career arc: a championship (2015) and two runner-up finishes (2013, 2016) suggest a manager who shows up big in January, and a 10-6 career playoff record backs that up — yet his overall record sits under .500 (84-103). Part of the story is Steve, who's beaten him 16 times in 23 meetings, including handing him the wrong end of the league's all-time highest-scoring game back in 2019. But Tim's still dangerous — a 189-point week as recently as 2025 shows he's not done making noise, and a 12-7 career edge on Luke keeps at least one rivalry firmly in his favor.

# Mark

Mark is the WFFL's answer to "always a bridesmaid, never a bride" — three runner-up finishes (2015, 2018, 2021) and zero championships across 14 seasons, the most title-game appearances without ever closing one out. Bryan's given him a particularly hard time too, an 8-14-1 record that's Mark's worst rivalry in the league. But he's no pushover: a 12-6 mark against Ken and a 176-point outburst in 2018 show he can still put a beating on someone when the matchup's right. At some point the math says a title has to break his way.

# Justin

Justin has a genuinely bizarre split personality: a 6-5 career playoff record, a level most managers would kill for, against an 83-102 regular-season mark that's one of the rougher ones in the league — somehow, a championship (2020) still snuck out of that combination. If there's a nemesis in Justin's story, it's Ted, who's beaten him 13 times in 15 meetings, the single most one-sided rivalry involving anyone still in the league. But he still swings hard — a 167.5-point week in 2025 is proof he can do damage when the matchups line up, and a 13-7 career edge on Lefty is a rivalry he actually wins.

# Lefty

Lefty took a three-year sabbatical from the league and came back to post the best winning percentage of anyone in the WFFL — 85-62-1, good for a .574 clip that edges out even Bryan's all-time-wins résumé. The receipts back it up: a fresh championship in 2025, a runner-up finish in 2023, and three third-place trips scattered across two different eras of his career. He's also tied for the league's longest win streak at nine games. The one gap: a 71-183 embarrassment against Ken in 2018 remains one of the worst single-game losses on record — but with a title banner freshly hung, nobody's bringing that up anymore.

# Dave

Dave opened his WFFL career about as well as anyone possibly could — back-to-back championships in 2013 and 2014, right out of the gate. He's stayed a plus scorer ever since (118.9 points per game, comfortably above league average) and owns a strong 12-4 record against Luke. The one number that'll follow him forever: his 32-150 loss to Ted in 2018 is both the fewest points ever scored in a single game and the biggest blowout in league history, a rough night nobody's letting him forget. Lefty's also had his number lifetime (16-5), the most lopsided rivalry either of them is on the wrong side of. Still, two rings from the league's first three years is a start most managers never catch up to.

# Lou

Lou has the distinction of being the WFFL's very first champion, back in 2012 — and 14 seasons later he's still right in the mix, with two more runner-up finishes since (2022, 2025) and a dead-even 94-93 career record. His 2025 demolition of Bryan (191.15-90.86, a 100-point margin) was one of the most lopsided results anyone's posted all era. A 14-7 record against Luke is his best rivalry by a mile, though Lefty's given him fits lifetime (11-4 the other way). Thirteen years after his first title, Lou's proven he was never a one-hit wonder — just patient.
